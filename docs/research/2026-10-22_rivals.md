## HEADLINE: a direct water-level rival appeared (Oct 20)
**StageSight**, https://github.com/Waqar-msx9t/stagesight (17 commits, all dated Oct 20; looks like a bulk import). The author appears to be a PhD (Muhammad Waqar Saleem, per its video script).
- OpenCV 5 reads **river stage off the staff plate** of a single **USGS gauge camera**: Hancock NY, USGS 01421500. Images come from the **USGS NIMS API** (`api.waterdata.usgs.gov/nims/v0`, the source behind HIVIS). It finds the plate marks with an HSV mask, connectedComponents and fitLine, and sets the datum with `cv.dnn.TextRecognitionModel` OCR, so it needs **no gauge data**.
- Accuracy on the 29 Jul 2026 flood: **RMSE 0.26 ft (bootstrap CI 0.20–0.31), 29/30 frames within 0.5 ft**. Baselines: plate fitted to the gauge 0.37 ft; linear pixel fit 6.31 ft. On a second event (4–5 Sep): RMSE 0.34 ft.
- The **Bedrock Claude agent** checks the camera against the gauge, decides which instrument is wrong, and queues sensor flags and flood alerts for **human approval, then sends SNS e-mail**. It ran fault-injection replays (offset, silent, frozen, drift, debris, spray), each **3× on Opus 4.6 and 3× on Sonnet 4.6** against a rule baseline. Over 90 days of no-fault operation it raised 0 false flags.
- **COOL** (Table II): c8g.4xlarge reads a 90-day archive of 1,869 frames in 4.1 s for about $0.0008. COOL is only 4% faster than pip in one process, but 4.9× faster on connectedComponentsWithStats.
- **Live Lambda Function URL dashboard (verified)**: flood replay, approval queue, agent log and a "try the reader" upload. It also has a LaTeX technical report PDF. The video is only a script in `docs/VIDEO_SCRIPT.md` (not recorded yet, unverified). I found **no Devpost page** for it (the Devpost search hit "StageSight" is an unrelated project).
- **Weaknesses**: **one camera only**. It needs a red-marked plate, daylight and water on the plate, so it **returns a number for only 2.4% of hourly frames**. There is one flood event, and the camera faults are synthetic.
- Threat: **5/5 in our lane**. It makes the same pitch as RiverSight (camera stage vs USGS gauge, plus an agent) with careful statistics.

## Implications for RiverSight
1. **Differentiate from StageSight directly.** Run a **fleet of many HIVIS cameras**, work **without a staff plate**, cover night and low water, and report coverage. StageSight returns a number for 2.4% of frames at one site. Put a "% of frames with a stage estimate × number of sites" table on page one.
2. **Match its statistical rigor**: per-camera RMSE in ft with bootstrap CIs, held-out flood events, its "plate fitted to gauge" and "linear pixel fit" baselines, and ideally its Hancock 01421500 site as a head-to-head row.
3. **The agent should do something StageSight's cannot.** Fleet-level triage across cameras, recalibration and drift detection, approval-gated alerts, and a fault-injection evaluation repeated N times. Their camera-vs-gauge disagreement idea is now table stakes.
4. **COOL**: publish an honest per-kernel plus end-to-end A/B on Graviton4 with $/1k frames. A fleet batch is the best case for COOL throughput, so show the scaling curve.
5. **Ship before Oct 26**: a live URL, a **≤4-minute video**, a judges section, and a "not claimed" section. With 4 days left, StageSight still lacks a video and a Devpost page, and that window is our opening.
