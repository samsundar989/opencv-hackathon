What we claim, and what we don't

We claim: a fleet-scale, plate-free, survey-free system. Each camera calibrates itself from its own USGS gauge history, reads stage when the gauge is down (gap-fill), reports flood state, flags its own problems (view change, lens, disagreement), and shows what is under water at any stage.

We do not claim: that the camera replaces a working gauge. While the gauge is online, the camera does not beat 15-minute persistence at any site. We also make no night readings (v1), no discharge, and no accuracy on cameras outside the 20 evaluated. Year-long cross-validation is harder than the 10 test days (median 0.38 ft vs 0.18 ft) because seasons drift.

Failure cases (shown, not hidden)

  • View changes: 8 of 20 cameras have periods that can't be registered to the current view. Registration catches it (VIEW_CHANGE), and the agent re-anchors or quarantines the site. Look for view changed on the live page.
  • Out-of-range stages: a live model cannot read above its calibrated range. Such readings are flagged OUT_OF_RANGE, shown as "≥ X ft (above calibrated range)", and the gauge is trusted. At Hancock the July flood peaked at 18.1 ft against a 12.0 ft calibration ceiling, and the frozen-protocol model failed there (report §6.6).
  • Night: no camera reading at night (IR frames). Gaps are shown, not filled.
